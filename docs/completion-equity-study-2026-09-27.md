# Separate US equity and revenue research study — 27 September 2026

This is an offline, fractional USD stock simulation. It does not evaluate NIFTY options, intraday scalping, Kotak execution, or a deployable strategy. All settings below were fixed before the observed outcomes were inspected. The results make no profit or qualification claim.

## Inputs and point-in-time rules

The local, Git-ignored source cache contains eight Yahoo Finance daily chart histories: AAPL, MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, JPM, XOM, JNJ, and PG, each with 2,514 adjusted closes from 26 September 2016 through 25 September 2026. The price request pattern is `https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/{symbol}?interval=1d&range=10y`. Its manifest has the exact URL, retrieval time, byte SHA-256, and expected count for each symbol. The raw chart timestamp marks a session, so the normalizer assigns the price to 16:00 America/New_York on that session date and excludes sessions after the requested as-of time. All portfolio prices use the adjusted-close series; raw OHLC values are never mixed into the strategy math. The eight files were retrieved from 15:07:47 to 15:07:55 UTC on 27 September 2026. The price manifest SHA-256 is `e7a37f3cb03f4fd043d092608d14fd167129befd91e1d874fdb27f0af9c50a88`.

The separate revenue example uses 48 [official Microsoft quarterly earnings releases](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/Investor/earnings/FY-2025-Q4/press-release-webcast), FY2015 Q1 through FY2026 Q4, retrieved from 15:07:49 to 15:08:47 UTC on 27 September 2026. The revenue manifest SHA-256 is `426f6dc1b8ca1e81e82e04ba94580439307bb9cea8bc7b7d63d1b6b0aaf0cf4b`. It stores each exact release URL, retrieval time, and byte hash. The parser takes the publication date and quarter end from the release header, confirms the current-year column in the quarterly income statement, and extracts consolidated revenue in USD millions. It independently reproduced all 48 previously extracted facts, including FY2015 Q3 at $21,729 million and FY2025 Q4 at $76,441 million. Segment rows and rounded headlines are excluded. Because the release headers omit time of day, a fact first becomes available at **midnight New York time on the next calendar day**. Quarter end itself never acts as publication time. Later information is as-of joined only after availability; the existing research adapter also prevents an older-quarter amendment from replacing the latest known quarter.

The cache loader verifies hashes, metadata, source hosts, count, ordering, and positive values. Missing files may be fetched only through bounded HTTPS reads; a present cache file with a wrong hash stops the run and is never overwritten. The raw vendor files and derived full ledgers remain in Git-ignored `data/research/`.

## Fixed experiment

Both asset rule families use adjusted closes only. The SMA family buys on a 5/34 moving-average positive crossing and sells on a negative crossing. The Bollinger family buys below a 20-day mean minus two sample standard deviations and sells above the upper band. These are daily long-only stock interpretations of the original Conlan mathematics, not option signal implementations. The shared portfolio engine ranks candidates by trailing 100-observation Sharpe, permits up to three fractional positions, decides from the preceding observed close, and fills at the next observed close. It starts with unit equity and liquidates remaining positions at the final close. Gross uses zero instrument cost. Base charges **10 bps per side**; stress charges **30 bps per side** on both buy and sell. These are scenario assumptions for US shares, not Kotak option fees. Each trade ledger reconciles gross price P&L less its entry and exit fees to net P&L.

The passive comparison purchases equal fractional stakes in all eight stocks at the first observed close and sells them at the final close, with the same per-side fee convention. The alternative-data comparison also includes a passive MSFT purchase over its exact evaluation portfolio window. These conventions omit spread, slippage, taxes, financing, market impact, and constraints on fractional shares.

For each rule family, 100 independent white-noise preference rankings and 100 trailing-window bootstrap preference rankings were evaluated with the same signals and portfolio rules under base and stress fees. A prespecified exploratory grid covered Sharpe windows 50/100/150 by maximum positions 1/3/5, nine configurations per family. Grid observations were not used to tune or reselect the declared reference. Every reference, control, grid, passive, and revenue-event portfolio has a full trade and equity ledger.

The Microsoft alternative-data model used the existing release-aware features, CUSUM threshold 5 on the observed trailing-year log revenue change, +2%/-1% close barriers over 90 calendar days, a chronological 70/30 event split, label-overlap purge, inverse-concurrency weights, and a fixed 100-tree forest with seed 42. The 64 feature-complete events yielded 44 training events and 20 evaluation events; no training event overlapped the evaluation start. All 20 evaluation labels were known. This is an event diagnostic on one company, not an executable trade predictor.

The rule portfolios grew in this rising, survivor-selected universe, yet both trailed the passive basket by a wide margin after scenario fees. Their reference preference ranks also failed to stand out from random controls. For the SMA family, 53.5% of white-noise controls and 63.4% of bootstrap controls matched or beat its 165.22% base return (including the finite-sample `+1` convention). The corresponding Bollinger fractions were 95.0% and 81.2%. The control median base returns were 178.99%/193.97% for SMA white noise/bootstrap and 324.01%/256.25% for Bollinger. The nine-grid base returns ranged 104.31–499.53% for SMA and 144.07–301.65% for Bollinger; the wide range makes post-result choice particularly prone to selection bias. No grid winner is advanced as a strategy.

The Microsoft event classifier achieved 50.0% accuracy on 20 known labels, equal to its training-majority baseline of 50.0%. It predicted the positive class for all 20 evaluation events. Thus its 29.97% base portfolio return came largely from buying and continuing to hold MSFT after the first positive event; it is not evidence of useful revenue timing. The matched passive MSFT return is close, and its small difference also reflects the different first fill close.

The historical eight names were chosen with present knowledge, so survivorship, selection, and sector concentration affect every result. The adjusted-close vendor series is a present-day adjustment vintage; historical dividend/split adjustments and later vendor corrections may not have been known at an earlier decision point. The Microsoft release pages are current retrieved versions, not archived publication-time snapshots; although Microsoft labels release files as of their earnings date, later site changes and accounting comparability remain possible. Only one firm's quarterly revenue was modeled. The 20-event evaluation is small, and a single chronological split does not establish stability. Fees are explicit scenarios rather than broker quotes. None of this evidence transfers to NIFTY option profitability or meets forward/live qualification requirements.
