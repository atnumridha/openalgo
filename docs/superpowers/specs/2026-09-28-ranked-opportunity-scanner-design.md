# Ranked opportunity scanner and Kotak-style option baskets

Date: 2026-09-28
Status: Design for operator review; no implementation or live orders authorized by this document.

## Intent and boundaries

The owner wants every *enabled* managed strategy to consider multiple current trade opportunities, approximately once per second, then automatically enter the highest-ranked eligible opportunities in its chosen Sandbox or Live mode as budget and risk allow. The 16 option-basket shapes visible in the supplied Kotak Neo screenshots join the same process. The selector must not promise profitability, manufacture signals, bypass existing risk controls, or turn a saved strategy into an armed strategy. The implementation is in OpenAlgo's managed Strategy Module; direct broker orders, arbitrary Python strategies, and ordinary Flow order nodes remain outside its scope.

"Enabled" means an armed managed strategy or its active scheduled/signal monitor. A saved, stopped, disabled, or uninstalled strategy is not scanned. Each strategy retains its own configured mode, broker/account pin, session window, signal logic, and exit rules. Installing a basket leaves it stopped and defaults to Sandbox. Switching to Live and arming it remain separate, owner-initiated operations using existing authorization and research-release gates. Deployment must not silently arm or change the mode of any existing strategy.

## Chosen architecture and alternatives

Use one owner- and mode-scoped opportunity coordinator within the Strategy Module. Strategy-specific candidate providers produce immutable proposals; a common ranker compares fresh proposals; the existing managed admission and execution path decides whether each proposal may enter. Extend the existing feed, order, and risk abstractions where necessary. Do not create a second order executor or a new Redis-based service for this feature.

Rejected alternatives: (1) one independent scanner/executor per strategy would race for cash, duplicate exposure, and broker calls; (2) REST polling every possible contract every second would overwhelm the quote path and age observations before a scan completes. Kotak documents streaming market data and batched quotes, but observed local broker behavior has rejected a full 50-symbol quote request. The scanner therefore uses bounded subscriptions and only a paced, degraded REST fallback; it never interprets a fallback as fresh when it is not.

## Market universe and cadence

Supported discovery domains are tradable, instrument-master-verified NSE F&O, BSE F&O, and MCX F&O on the connected Kotak account. A strategy may narrow its own allowed exchanges, underlyings, expiries, times, premium or price band, spread, volume/open-interest, and maximum candidate count. Existing strategy configuration and exchange-session restrictions take precedence. Discovery refreshes at a slower bounded cadence and on expiry/contract changes; it is not a full-chain REST request every second. The live feed maintains a bounded shortlist of underlying and contract quotes with exchange timestamps, bid/ask sizes, and feed health. Subscription caps and request pacing are configurable and conservative; unknown or unverified caps cause a smaller universe or no entry, never unlimited calls.

The decision loop targets one evaluation per second for *currently fresh* data. This is a scheduling target, not a promise of one broker update or trade per second. A cycle may yield no candidate. A stale, crossed, incomplete, or temporally inconsistent quote; disconnected feed; unavailable option chain; missing contract valuation multiplier; rate limit; or missing cost schedule removes the affected candidate. If the entire feed or ledger is uncertain, no new entries occur; exits and reconciliation continue. Observed tick timestamps, receipt timestamps, discovery version, and exclusion reasons are recorded. No synthetic quote can qualify a live entry.

## Candidate providers and the basket catalog

Each enabled strategy supplies candidates through a small provider interface: `(strategy, signal/context, market snapshot) -> candidate proposals or reasons for none`. Existing indicators, closed-bar requirements, external-signal direction, ML release bindings, and freshness windows stay authoritative. A receiver without its required signal produces no candidate. Providers may compare multiple valid contracts or markets *within that strategy's permitted universe*; they may not invent an entry merely because another symbol looks active. Non-option strategies use the same interface for their supported instruments. During staged Sandbox migration, an adapter not yet capable of multiple valid proposals retains its existing behavior, is shown as `single-candidate`, and is not falsely labeled multi-opportunity-ready. Release cannot claim the requested all-enabled-strategy behavior until every supported enabled family has an adapter and participates in ranking; an unranked fallback cannot silently enter Live.

The basket catalog contains 16 static **leg topologies**, not proprietary Kotak entry or exit signals. Categories and shapes are: bullish—Buy Call, Bull Call Spread, Bull Put Spread, Call Ratio Spread; bearish—Buy Put, Bear Put Spread, Bear Call Spread, Put Ratio Spread; neutral—Short Straddle, Short Strangle, Long Call Butterfly, Short Iron Condor; volatile—Long Straddle, Long Strangle, Long Iron Butterfly, Long Iron Condor. Twelve detail layouts are visible in the supplied screenshots. Bear Put Spread, Bear Call Spread, Put Ratio Spread, and Long Strangle use conventional inferred layouts, visibly labeled as inferred rather than verified Kotak defaults. A template is available for installation, but it proposes a trade only when the owner has configured an explicit entry criterion or strategy-specific signal. Static payoff shape alone is not an entry signal. Basket scanning needs an explicit armed-monitor lifecycle; calling the existing batch `start` route would place an immediate trade and is not the implementation of arming.

For each proposal, resolve all legs against the same valid underlying, expiry, exchange and current instrument master. Preserve side, option type, strike ordering, and lot ratios (including 1:2 ratio spreads and 1:2:1 butterflies); never reuse the screenshot's dated strike or lot-size values. Reject invalid, missing, or changed legs as a complete proposal. The UI shows the source strategy, market, resolved legs, provenance, mode, expected debit/credit, modeled costs, stop/target plan, margin evidence, and risk/rejection reason.

## Ranking and admission

The candidate contract contains owner, strategy/rule version, mode, broker pin, signal identity, resolved legs, quote timestamps, and evidence for price, liquidity, costs, reward and planned loss. The ranker uses a deterministic, versioned *relative* score from comparable strategy-signal strength, net reward-to-planned-risk and execution quality. Missing signal-strength calibration cannot be replaced with a fabricated win probability; such providers either supply a documented rule-strength scale or remain in a separately disclosed unranked/single-candidate path until adapted. Ties use stable identifiers. Scores describe rule fit, not expected profit or probability of success.

Within each owner and mode, fresh proposals are ranked. The coordinator attempts the top candidate first, then lower-ranked candidates while the current budget and portfolio governor permit. It prevents duplicate signal/contract entries and conflicting exposures, and uses an owner-scoped lease so concurrent workers cannot admit the same opportunity twice. Recheck freshness, account state, mode authorization, available funds, working orders, risk, costs, and broker pin under the admission lease immediately before dispatch. Once an entry changes budget or positions, recompute eligibility for remaining proposals. Sandbox and Live have separate queues and ledgers. Candidates not admitted are *recomputed on the next cycle*, never persisted as executable backlog. Cooldowns, open-position caps, daily stop, drawdown pause, and session cutoff remain hard gates.

## Risk and multi-leg execution

The existing governor, allocated-capital profile, session-loss accounting, research/live authorization, broker-held stops, and unknown-order fail-closed behavior are authoritative. This is not a route around them. Current allocated-capital documentation and code support only single-leg long option entries; the live-protection path requires a fixed stop for every leg. Therefore multi-leg or short-option proposals must be blocked from Live—and from any managed-risk mode that cannot value them—until a tested extension can compute conservative basket and per-leg exposure, charges, slippage, broker margin, contract monetary multipliers, and protective exits without relaxing the existing ceilings. This extension is part of the implementation plan, not an exception to policy. Absence of basket-aware broker margin evidence blocks Live. Unlimited-loss payoff tails receive an explicit warning and conservative stress/exposure treatment; if no finite, policy-compliant planned-loss bound can be established, the entry is blocked even when the owner selected Live. A stop is not a guaranteed maximum realized loss.

Dispatch protective/risk-reducing legs first where possible, but never assume exchange-level basket atomicity. Track each leg's intended, accepted, partial, filled, cancelled, and unknown quantity using durable order and position references. On a partial basket, halt further entries, cancel outstanding entry remainders, reconcile actual broker positions and stop ownership, then flatten or otherwise protect known exposure according to the existing exit rules. An unknown outcome holds its reservation and blocks new entries until reconciled. A failed protective order is a critical alert and cannot be treated as a successful entry. Exits stay available even when entry scanning is paused.

Existing single-leg strategies keep their current stops and targets. New basket entry criteria and stops/targets must be configured and previewed; no values or profitability claims are reverse-engineered from payoff screenshots. Risk checks must use executable bounded prices, whole lots, current costs and the same plan submitted to the broker. If a rule conflicts with current risk policy or cannot be proven, the candidate is rejected rather than resized into a different trade without the owner's saved sizing policy.

## Operator experience and rollout

Expose scanner health, mode, armed strategies, discovery coverage, quote age, ranked candidates, admission decisions, and rejection reasons in Strategy Module review. The operator can set allowed universe/ranges, enable individual basket templates, stop scanning, and use the existing kill switch. A visible mode badge distinguishes Sandbox from Live. Template installation does not create or activate a scheduled Flow; a manual basket uses the managed start/arm lifecycle and shared coordinator. Existing Flow signal producers remain usable. API responses must not imply an order or position exists merely because a candidate was ranked or an order requested.

Roll out in stages: pure candidate/ranking and replay tests; Sandbox scanner with existing strategy adapters and baskets; fault/partial-fill validation; then Live-capable code path with mocked broker integration. Real Live arming remains blocked until that strategy's risk and protection support, research-release requirement (if configured), and owner authorization all pass. No production order, live mode switch, blanket strategy arm, account-risk setting change, or risk-limit increase is part of implementation or testing. Any eventual real-money smoke test is a separate owner action.

## Verification and acceptance

- Parameterized topology and payoff tests cover all 16 templates, ratios, direction, strike order, dynamic expiry/lot changes, and the four inferred-provenance labels.
- Replay tests drive every enabled managed strategy family through multiple simultaneous opportunities, deterministic ranks, budget-limited multiple admissions, duplicate prevention, separate Sandbox/Live ledgers, and fresh re-ranking rather than stale queue execution.
- Contract tests cover NSE/BSE/MCX units, session boundaries, option-chain churn, bounded subscriptions and quote pacing, stale/crossed/missing data, feed reconnects, and rate-limit backoff.
- Risk tests prove unchanged ceilings and approval gates, no entry without costs/stops/margin/multiplier evidence, no naked-live shortcut, serialized admissions across workers, and correct reservations after partial, late, rejected, or unknown broker outcomes.
- End-to-end Sandbox tests exercise candidate → admission → order/fill → protective exit and audit/UI status. Mocked Live tests exercise the same path without sending real broker orders. Existing strategy regression suites, TypeScript/UI tests, and a risk-focused code review must pass before any readiness claim.
- Success means enabled strategies can compare multiple current opportunities and admit only fresh, policy-compliant top-ranked matches at the target cadence. It does **not** mean a trade occurs every second, every basket is live-eligible, or any strategy is profitable.

## Explicit exclusions

No reverse engineering of Kotak proprietary signal, stop, POP, or backtest algorithms; no promise of profit; no direct broker-account mutation during development; no automatic enablement of saved strategies; no widening of risk limits; no guaranteed atomic multi-leg fill; and no full-market, unbounded one-second REST polling.
